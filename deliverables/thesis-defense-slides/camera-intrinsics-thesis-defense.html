01Introduction — Motivation

A single image does not determine scale

Two different worlds can cast the exact same image.

  • Under pinhole projection, image position depends only on the ratio X⁄Z — a bigger object twice as far away is pixel-for-pixel indistinguishable from a smaller, closer one.
  • Monocular depth models resolve this ambiguity implicitly, from learned priors about object size and perspective — priors that are wrong exactly when the scene doesn't match training statistics.
  • The camera's intrinsic matrix K is metadata nearly every real camera already exposes, and it resolves this ambiguity explicitly and exactly — yet foundation depth models never see it.
02Introduction — Related Work

From hand-crafted cues to foundation models

Classical geometric cues

Hoiem et al. · Make3D (Saxena et al.) — convergent lines, texture, horizon. Limit: brittle scene-structure assumptions, poor generalization.

Supervised deep CNNs

Eigen et al. 2014 / 2015 — multi-scale, multi-task depth CNNs. Limit: learned scale tied to the training camera; evaluated mostly on normalized metrics.

Self-supervised stereo

Monodepth, Monodepth2 (Godard et al.) — view synthesis, Z=fb/d. Limit: scale comes from a fixed stereo baseline b, not from K.

Self-supervised video / SfM

Zhou et al. 2017 — K used only inside the warping function. Limit: the depth network itself never sees K; ambiguity remains.

Relative / foundation models

MiDaS, DPT, Depth Anything V2 (this thesis's base) — excellent relative depth at scale. Limit: output is inherently non-metric.

Explicit-geometry outliers

Saxena et al. 2023 — FoV-conditioned diffusion (costly inference); Chen et al. 2022 — camera-aware MVS (needs multiple views, not single-image).

03Introduction — The Gap

Four ways to handle K — almost everyone picks the first three

1
Ignored entirely — classical geometric-cue and most supervised CNN methods.
2
Implicitly absorbed into weights — assumes K is constant across training and test; most relative/foundation models, including base Depth Anything V2.
3
Used only inside the cost function — self-supervised warping (Zhou et al.); the depth network itself never receives K as input.
4
Explicit input conditioning — the network's forward pass literally reads K. Rare (Chen et al. 2022 for multi-view MVS; Guizilini et al. use calibration during training but not as a systematic input). This thesis's position — for single-image, foundation-model depth.
04Introduction — Research Questions

Three questions this thesis answers

RQ1

Do common monocular depth models behave stably as the dataset and the camera's intrinsic parameters change?

RQ2

Can non-metric evaluation metrics conceal instabilities that exist in the physical scale of predicted depth?

RQ3

Can incorporating explicit camera-intrinsics improve metric depth estimation and reduce a model's sensitivity to changes in the camera?

A hidden fourth requirement

Answering RQ3 with a benchmark win isn't enough — a win could be spurious. The thesis adds a causal-validity requirement: prove the model actually reads K, not merely correlates with it. That requirement is what the K-sweep and K-knockout tests in §III exist to satisfy.

02Method — Camera Geometry

The pinhole model and K

A 3D point (X,Y,Z) projects to pixel (u,v) through the intrinsic matrix K — focal lengths fx,fy and principal point cx,cy. Every quantity a depth model must recover — absolute scale, object size in metres — is entangled with exactly these four numbers.

known to the camera at capture time hidden from foundation depth models today
u=fx·X⁄Z+cx
v=fy·Y⁄Z+cy
K = fx00 0fy0 cxcy1
03Method — Camera Descriptor

A normalized, 9-dimensional camera descriptor

Raw (fx,fy,cx,cy) is resolution-dependent and hard for a network to consume directly — it is expanded into a normalized descriptor that also encodes field of view and image shape.

d(K,H,W) = [ f̃x, f̃y, c̃x, c̃y, FoVw, FoVh, W⁄H, (fx+fy)⁄2, H·W ]

What each term contributes

Focal lengths & principal point (normalized) give raw calibration; field-of-view angles give a resolution-independent notion of "how wide is this shot"; aspect ratio and mean focal length help the encoder relate K to typical camera families it may have seen.

The coordinate-frame constraint

K and (H,W) must describe the same image frame — crop or resize the image without updating K to match, and the descriptor becomes geometrically wrong. This exact mistake is Bug #2 in the campaign, §III.

04Method — Architecture

A gated camera token, injected into the DINOv2 encoder

Zero-initialized gate

g starts at exactly 0, so the camera token contributes nothing at step 0 — fine-tuning begins identical to the pretrained baseline and opens the gate only if K reduces the loss.

Frozen DINOv2 backbone

Only the DPT head and the camera encoder adapt (lr 5e-6, head 10× multiplier) — this preserves the ~62M-image pretrained prior and avoids the catastrophic zero-shot drift discussed in §III.

04Method — Design Rationale

Why inject a token — not concatenate, not FiLM?

Three ways to condition a pretrained vision transformer on an external vector, compared against the constraint that matters most here: the pretrained weights must stay usable from step zero.

(a) Channel concatenation

Append K as extra input-image channels.

Corrupts the pretrained first layer — its weights were never fit to this channel count.

(b) FiLM-style modulation

Learn γ,β from K to rescale/shift feature maps directly (Perez et al. 2018).

Requires direct feature-map manipulation throughout the backbone — harder to gate to exactly zero everywhere at once.

(c) Token + attention used

Encode K as one extra token in the sequence; let self-attention decide how much it matters.

Pretrained weights untouched — with the gate at zero, training can start from a fully frozen encoder.

05Method — Training Design

Every result is a matched pair, not a single number

Two models are fine-tuned from the same released Depth Anything V2 checkpoint, on the same data, seed, and schedule — differing only in whether the camera branch is present.

  • Comparing proposed vs. released DA2 answers "is the overall system better?"
  • Comparing proposed vs. matched control — which differ only in access to K — isolates whether K specifically is the reason.
  • Both comparisons are reported for every experiment in this talk.
06Method — Optimization

Two training regimes, two loss families

relative / "basic" models

Scale-shift-invariant loss, on disparity

Predictions carry no inherent scale, so both training and evaluation first solve a per-image least-squares alignment D*=s·D+t, then combine a pixel term with a multi-scale gradient-matching term.

LSSI = (1−α)Lpixel + αLgrad  α≈0.5
Tdisp = 1⁄max(DGT, dmin)
metric models

Scale-Invariant Log loss (SiLog)

Predictions are absolute depth in metres, unaligned at evaluation time — this is the regime where a global-scale signal like K has somewhere to matter, as §III shows.

L = √Var(d) + λ·E[d]²

di = log ŷi − log yi; λ controls how much global-scale error is forgiven — the campaign varies it 0.5→0.3.

This is where the thesis's evidence actually comes from. Every early "win" turned out to be an artifact of a bug — finding and fixing three of them, honestly reporting two dead ends, and then designing a targeted causal test is the real methodological contribution.

Fine-tuning a foundation model trained on ~62M images, on a few thousand small/synthetic images, destroys zero-shot robustness regardless of the camera-intrinsics input — 0.2139 vs. 0.2132 is noise. 11Campaign — The Pivot

Switch to the protocol under which "better" is provable

Fine-tune on a benchmark's own training split; evaluate on its held-out test split; compare against the released model evaluated identically — the same practice used by BTS, AdaBins, ZoeDepth, and DA2's own Table 4.

train.py --encoder {vits|vitl} --datasets nyu --model-type {basic|metric} --min-depth 0.001 --max-depth {10|20} --epochs 40 --lr 5e-6 --freeze-dinov2 --head-lr-multiplier 10.0 --grad-clip 1.0 [proposed: --model-variant revised --use-camera-intrinsics] [control: --model-variant base]
  • Start from the released checkpoint, not from scratch — preserves the pretrained prior.
  • Frozen backbone, lr 5e-6 — only the head and camera encoder adapt, preventing the drift seen on the previous slide.

13Campaign — Bug #2
2

ViT-L silently collapsed to zero

Scaling the identical recipe to ViT-L froze validation AbsRel at 0.346 from epoch 1 — the model learned to output exactly zero everywhere. The loss curve looked completely normal throughout.

Root cause

The pretrained model outputs disparity (inverse depth, values in the hundreds); training fed it direct depth targets (0–10 m). The loss's alignment scale is clamped positive (min=1e-3), so it cannot fit an inverted relationship — and its gradient-matching term crushes the output toward zero, which is unrecoverable once the head's terminal ReLU is dead.

Tdisp = 1⁄max(DGT, dmin)
the fix — train on inverse-depth targets, matching the pretrained model's own output convention
0.001→1.0
dead-pixel fraction, within 18 steps
20–50×
lower loss after fix, verified before committing further GPU time

Why the tie? A capacity × data hypothesis

NYU is captured with a single Kinect sensor — K is constant across the entire training set, so the camera encoder has no variation to learn a mapping from. Worse: the existing pipeline randomly crops images without updating K to match — the encoder was training on values that were constant and wrong. This motivates the next fix.

15Campaign — A Conceptual Turning Point

Give K real variation — the fix helps everyone, but not via K

A zoom-crop augmentation resamples a random window and updates K exactly:

cx′=cx−x0,  cy′=cy−y0
fx′=fx·Wnet⁄wwin,  fy′=fy·Hnet⁄hwin

Why — a structural blind spot in the evaluation, not the model

Relative/"basic" evaluation aligns scale-and-shift per image before scoring — which removes exactly the global metric scale that a focal length disambiguates. At ViT-L capacity the visual encoder already extracts strong scale cues from content alone; the camera token has nothing left to add under this protocol.

16Campaign — Bug #3
3

A silent ~4× depth error at evaluation time

Moving to metric evaluation, the loader constructed the released checkpoint's sigmoid×max-depth head class for checkpoints that were actually trained with train.py's unified ReLU head. Identical parameter names and shapes meant the weights loaded with no error — but produced depths about 4× too large (eigen AbsRel 2.97 instead of ≈0.08).

Fixed — checkpoints now record from_train_py in their metadata; evaluation routes to the correct head architecture automatically

18Campaign — The Key Idea

Make scale mathematically unrecoverable from pixels alone

From the pinhole equation itself: scaling focal length by α and every scene depth by α produces the identical image — u=fX/Z is invariant under (f,Z)→(αf,αZ).

α ~ exp(U(−ln m, +ln m))  m=1.6

multiplies fx, fy and GT depth by the same α per sample — geometrically exact, zero interpolation artifacts, zero information leaked through the pixels.

What this does to the K-blind control

It now faces literally identical pixels paired with contradictory depth labels across training samples. The best it can do is regress to the mean of the α distribution — an irreducible error floor, by construction.

What this does to the K-aware model

It can resolve α exactly by reading the (now-jittered) focal length off its own input. Using K stops being merely helpful and becomes mathematically necessary to avoid the control's failure mode.

seed-locked pairs — bit-exact iteration-0 loss (1.725 = 1.725), so any difference is attributable only to K

20Campaign — Mechanism, Visually

The control's error is exactly the α noise it can't resolve

Per-image ratio of predicted to ground-truth depth (median), focal-jitter round, all 654 eigen-test images

Reading the two distributions

The control distribution is both biased away from 1.0 and visibly wider (median 1.041, sd 0.094 vs 0.078) — it is doing exactly what a model regressing to the mean of an unresolvable random variable should do.

The proposed model's tight, centered distribution is direct visual evidence that reading K lets it resolve α on a per-image basis, not just on average.

23Campaign — Mechanistic Proof

The K-sweep: rescale f at inference, watch the output move

20 test images; fx, fy scaled by {0.6, 0.8, 1.0, 1.25, 1.6}; log–log slope of predicted depth vs. injected scale. Ideal slope = 1.0 (exact pinhole rescaling); slope = 0 means K is ignored entirely.

24Campaign — Causal Proof

Withhold or corrupt K — accuracy degrades on cue

The round-3 proposed checkpoint, evaluated under four input conditions, full test set (n=654).

1. Withholding K is not decorative

AbsRel degrades 59% (0.092→0.147) when K is withheld — a substantial share of the model's scale computation flows through this pathway.

2. The degradation matches geometry, quantitatively

With measured slope s=0.93, the pinhole prediction for a wrong focal scale X is |Xs−1|: predicted ≈0.28 (X=0.7) and ≈0.37 (X=1.4) — observed 0.289 and 0.427. Not just "wrong K hurts" — hurts by the amount geometry says it should.

26Campaign — Case Study

CityScapes: what real, imperfect ground truth teaches

Real urban scenes, diverse lighting and weather, objects from near vehicles to distant buildings. Unlike NYU's active-sensor depth, CityScapes ground truth comes from stereo matching + 3D reconstruction — accurate near the camera on salient objects, but noisy and discontinuous in distant, weak-texture regions.

Why raw numbers here can mislead

RMSE and SiLog are especially sensitive to this GT noise; urban depth spans metres to tens of metres, so small relative errors become large absolute ones. A model that is more scale-stable will not necessarily look uniformly better on every metric here.

The explicit methodological stance

Direct numeric comparison of CityScapes results against other datasets is not treated as valid in this thesis. It is used strictly as a case study of model behaviour under complex, real, urban conditions — not as another row in a leaderboard.

IV

Discussion & Conclusion

Answering the research questions  ·  contributions  ·  limitations  ·  future work
01Discussion — Verdicts

Back to the three questions

RQ1

Are common models stable across dataset and camera change?

No. The baseline is measurably sensitive on metric metrics across datasets and camera parameters (camera audit, cross-dataset SiLog differences) — instability that is real but often invisible to normalized evaluation.

RQ2

Can non-metric metrics conceal that instability?

Yes. AbsRel is GT-normalized and often hides scale-related change; RMSE consistently reveals it. Seen directly in this campaign (RMSE decisive where AbsRel is n.s.) and in why CityScapes numbers resist naive comparison.

RQ3

Does explicit K improve metric depth and reduce camera-sensitivity?

Yes — decisively under metric evaluation, protocol- and capacity-dependent under the relative/basic protocol. Proven not just statistically but mechanistically (K-sweep) and causally (K-knockout) — satisfying the causal-validity requirement raised alongside the original RQs.

02Discussion — Contributions

Contributions — theoretical & practical

theoretical

1. Reframing scale ambiguity

As an inherent geometric limitation of the image→3D mapping — not a network or training weakness — that requires explicit camera geometry to resolve.

2. Metric vs. non-metric evaluation

A conceptual framework showing normalized-metric evaluation alone can give an incomplete, sometimes misleading, picture of true model performance.

practical

3. A minimal, compatible solution

Preserves the base architecture; adds K as one gated token — no large architectural change, no extra data collection required.

4. Works with a fully frozen encoder

Meaningful metric improvement achieved without touching the pretrained feature-extraction weights at all — a low-risk adoption path for practitioners.

04Discussion — Limitations

Limitations, stated precisely

  • 1Dependence on K's accuracy. K-knockout quantified this: full removal ≈59% AbsRel increase; 30–40% focal distortion causes several-times baseline error. Realistic (small, non-adversarial) calibration noise was not tested.
  • 2Ground-truth quality varies across datasets — generation method, depth range, and uniformity all differ, affecting absolute error interpretation (see CityScapes, §III).
  • 3Single base architecture. Implemented and evaluated only on Depth Anything V2; conceptually architecture-independent, but generalization to other encoders/decoders is untested.
  • 4Static, single-image scope. Temporal and multi-view geometric constraints are unused — a deliberate choice to isolate K's role, but it limits applicability.
  • 5The controlled study is NYU-only, with camera diversity simulated via augmentation rather than genuine multi-camera capture; round-2's AbsRel significance was borderline (p=0.040) even where RMSE was decisive; generalizing to outdoor, large-depth-range benchmarks (e.g. KITTI) is unstarted.
  • 6Single seed per arm. Seed-locking gives strong within-pair causal inference, but training-process variance across independent seeds was not separately estimated.
05Discussion — Future Work

Where this goes next

  • 1Robustness to imprecise K — study behaviour under realistic calibration noise; design uncertainty-aware handling.
  • 2Joint depth & intrinsics estimation — estimate or refine K jointly with depth, instead of assuming it's known.
  • 3Genuinely multi-camera training data — the dose-response pattern predicts an even larger, more stable K contribution; also sweep the token-injection layer and depth.
  • 4Multi-seed statistical robustness — repeat each paired arm across independent seeds, reporting mean/std alongside the paired image-level tests.
  • 5Multi-image / video — combine temporal and multi-view constraints with explicit K conditioning.
  • 6Architecture generalization — test whether the gains transfer to other encoder/decoder families, or are specific to this one.
