\enabstract{
Monocular depth estimation underpins applications such as robotics, autonomous driving, and 3D reconstruction, which require the physical distance of objects. A single image, however, does not determine the absolute scale of a scene, because the apparent size of an object depends on the camera's focal length as well as on its distance. Current foundation models recover the relative structure of a scene accurately, but they mainly predict relative depth, and their absolute (metric) versions usually do not receive the camera geometry as an explicit input; their error therefore grows when the camera or dataset changes.

In this thesis, the known camera calibration matrix is converted into a token by a transformer encoder and injected through a learnable gate into the DINOv2 encoder of Depth Anything V2. To measure the contribution of this input, the proposed model is compared with a control model that differs from it only in its access to this matrix.

In a controlled study on NYUv2 with focal-jitter augmentation, AbsRel falls from 0.1065 for the control model to 0.0834 for the proposed model (about 22 percent), and the depth predicted by the proposed model changes with the focal length almost as the pinhole model predicts. In the final step, a single model was trained jointly on four indoor and outdoor datasets captured with different cameras. Under absolute evaluation, the proposed model attains a lower AbsRel than the control model on all eight test sets with both the ViT-S and ViT-L encoders, while the two models remain close in accuracy under relative evaluation. With the ViT-L encoder, on NYUv2, Cityscapes, and data from two SUN RGB-D Kinect sensors not seen in training, the proposed model is more accurate than both the control model and the released absolute version of Depth Anything V2 in all three metrics, AbsRel, $\delta_1$, and RMSE; on NYUv2, for example, its AbsRel is 0.088, against 0.133 for the control model and 0.207 for the released version. A single model that receives the calibration matrix of each camera can therefore estimate absolute depth for different cameras without separate retraining or manual scale adjustment, and can reduce the need for active depth sensors in robotics and driver-assistance systems.
}
