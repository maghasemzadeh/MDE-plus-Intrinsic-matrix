\enabstract{
Monocular depth estimation (MDE) underpins robotics, autonomous driving, augmented reality, and 3D reconstruction, yet a single image does not uniquely determine the metric scale of a scene, and current foundation models mostly output relative depth that is insufficient for physical measurement. Methods such as CAM-Convs, Metric3D, and UniDepth mitigate this by feeding camera intrinsics to the network, but in all of them the intrinsics were introduced together with changes in architecture, data, and training schedule, so the contribution of the camera calibration matrix itself has not been isolated.

In this thesis, a normalized nine-dimensional descriptor of the camera calibration matrix is injected as a gated token into the DINOv2 encoder of Depth Anything V2, and the proposed model is compared with a control model that shares the same data, split, hyperparameters, and random seed and differs only in its access to the calibration matrix. Evaluation uses the 654-image Eigen test set of NYUv2. Under relative evaluation, per-image scale-and-shift alignment removes most of the information carried by the focal length, and the contribution of the calibration matrix is significant only for the ViT-S encoder; under metric evaluation without alignment, the proposed model consistently outperforms the control, and the margin grows with field-of-view diversity. With a geometrically exact focal-jitter augmentation that makes scale unrecoverable from pixels alone, AbsRel falls from 0.1030 for the control to 0.0849 for the proposed model (about an 18 percent reduction, $p\leq10^{-24}$). The log--log slope of predicted depth versus focal scale rises from 0.06 to 0.93--0.97 (ideal value 1), and withholding the calibration matrix increases AbsRel by 59 percent. Camera intrinsics therefore provide a statistically significant and causally necessary scale signal, although this reliance increases sensitivity to calibration accuracy, and generalization to genuinely multi-camera and outdoor data requires further study.
}
