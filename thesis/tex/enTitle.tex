\enabstract{
\begin{eddeleted}
\eddel{Monocular depth estimation (MDE) underpins robotics, autonomous driving, augmented reality, and 3D reconstruction, yet a single image does not uniquely determine the absolute scale of a scene, and current foundation models mostly output relative depth that is insufficient for physical measurement. Methods such as CAM-Convs, Metric3D, and UniDepth mitigate this by feeding camera intrinsics to the network, but none of the existing methods feeds the known intrinsics, without estimating them, as a token into the transformer encoder of a single-image foundation model: CAM-Convs adds them as coordinate channels to convolutions, Metric3D maps the input to a canonical camera space outside the network, and UniDepth and Depth Pro estimate the camera from the image itself. Moreover, in these methods the camera information was introduced together with changes in architecture, data, and training schedule, so the contribution of the camera calibration matrix itself has not been isolated.}

\eddel{In this thesis, a normalized nine-dimensional descriptor of the known camera calibration matrix is encoded by a transformer camera encoder and injected as a gated token into the transformer DINOv2 encoder of Depth Anything V2, and the proposed model is compared with a control model that shares the same data, split, hyperparameters, and random seed and differs only in its access to the calibration matrix. Evaluation uses the standard NYUv2 test split. Under relative evaluation, per-image scale-and-shift alignment removes most of the information carried by the focal length, and the contribution of the calibration matrix is significant only for the ViT-S encoder; under absolute evaluation without alignment, the proposed model consistently outperforms the control, and the margin grows with field-of-view diversity. With a geometrically exact focal-jitter augmentation that makes scale unrecoverable from pixels alone, AbsRel falls from 0.1065 for the control to 0.0834 for the proposed model (about a 22 percent reduction). The log--log slope of predicted depth versus focal scale rises from 0.06 to 0.97 (ideal value 1), and withholding the calibration matrix increases AbsRel by 111 percent. Camera intrinsics therefore provide a statistically significant and causally necessary scale signal, although this reliance increases sensitivity to calibration accuracy, and generalization to genuinely multi-camera and outdoor data requires further study.}

\eddel{As a first step in that direction, a single ViT-L model was trained jointly on NYUv2, KITTI, Virtual KITTI 2, and Cityscapes, covering four cameras and both indoor and outdoor scenes. On NYUv2, Cityscapes, and SUN RGB-D Kinect v1 and Kinect v2 data not used in training, the proposed model is more accurate than both the control model and the released metric Depth Anything V2 model in AbsRel, {$\delta_1$}, and RMSE; on NYUv2, for example, AbsRel is 0.088, against 0.133 for the control and 0.207 for the released model. The same multi-domain training was repeated for two encoder sizes, ViT-S and ViT-L, each as an absolute (metric) model and a relative model; in the absolute models, the proposed model was clearly more accurate than the control model at both sizes, whereas the relative models, evaluated after per-image scale alignment, showed no consistent gain.}
\end{eddeleted}
\begin{edadded}
Monocular depth estimation underpins applications such as robotics, autonomous driving, and 3D reconstruction, which require the physical distance of objects. A single image, however, does not determine the absolute scale of a scene, because the apparent size of an object depends on the camera's focal length as well as on its distance. Current foundation models recover the relative structure of a scene accurately, but they mainly predict relative depth, and their absolute (metric) versions usually do not receive the camera geometry as an explicit input; their error therefore grows when the camera or dataset changes.

In this thesis, the known camera calibration matrix is converted into a token by a transformer encoder and injected through a learnable gate into the DINOv2 encoder of Depth Anything V2. To measure the contribution of this input, the proposed model is compared with a control model that differs from it only in its access to this matrix.

In a controlled study on NYUv2 with focal-jitter augmentation, AbsRel falls from 0.1065 for the control model to 0.0834 for the proposed model (about 22 percent), and the depth predicted by the proposed model changes with the focal length almost as the pinhole model predicts. In the final step, a single model was trained jointly on four indoor and outdoor datasets captured with different cameras. Under absolute evaluation, the proposed model attains a lower AbsRel than the control model on all eight test sets with both the ViT-S and ViT-L encoders, while the two models remain close in accuracy under relative evaluation. With the ViT-L encoder, on NYUv2, Cityscapes, and data from two SUN RGB-D Kinect sensors not seen in training, the proposed model is more accurate than both the control model and the released absolute version of Depth Anything V2 in all three metrics, AbsRel, $\delta_1$, and RMSE; on NYUv2, for example, its AbsRel is 0.088, against 0.133 for the control model and 0.207 for the released version. A single model that receives the calibration matrix of each camera can therefore estimate absolute depth for different cameras without separate retraining or manual scale adjustment, and can reduce the need for active depth sensors in robotics and driver-assistance systems.
\end{edadded}
