\enabstract{
Monocular depth estimation (MDE) underpins robotics, autonomous driving, augmented reality, and 3D reconstruction, yet a single image does not uniquely determine the absolute scale of a scene, and current foundation models mostly output relative depth that is insufficient for physical measurement. Methods such as CAM-Convs, Metric3D, and UniDepth mitigate this by feeding camera intrinsics to the network, but none of the existing methods feeds the known intrinsics, without estimating them, as a token into the transformer encoder of a single-image foundation model: CAM-Convs adds them as coordinate channels to convolutions, Metric3D maps the input to a canonical camera space outside the network, UniDepth and Depth Pro estimate the camera from the image itself, and Depth Anything 3 uses a camera token only for cross-view consistency with multi-view input. Moreover, in these methods the camera information was introduced together with changes in architecture, data, and training schedule, so the contribution of the camera calibration matrix itself has not been isolated.

In this thesis, a normalized nine-dimensional descriptor of the known camera calibration matrix is encoded by a transformer camera encoder and injected as a gated token into the transformer DINOv2 encoder of Depth Anything V2, and the proposed model is compared with a control model that shares the same data, split, hyperparameters, and random seed and differs only in its access to the calibration matrix. Evaluation uses the standard NYUv2 test split. Under relative evaluation, per-image scale-and-shift alignment removes most of the information carried by the focal length, and the contribution of the calibration matrix is significant only for the ViT-S encoder; under absolute evaluation without alignment, the proposed model consistently outperforms the control, and the margin grows with field-of-view diversity. With a geometrically exact focal-jitter augmentation that makes scale unrecoverable from pixels alone, AbsRel falls from 0.1030 for the control to 0.0849 for the proposed model (about an 18 percent reduction). The log--log slope of predicted depth versus focal scale rises from 0.06 to 0.93--0.97 (ideal value 1), and withholding the calibration matrix increases AbsRel by 59 percent. Camera intrinsics therefore provide a statistically significant and causally necessary scale signal, although this reliance increases sensitivity to calibration accuracy, and generalization to genuinely multi-camera and outdoor data requires further study.

\edpar{As a first step in that direction, a single ViT-L model was trained jointly on NYUv2, KITTI, Virtual KITTI 2, and Cityscapes, covering four cameras and both indoor and outdoor scenes. On NYUv2, Cityscapes, and two SUN RGB-D sensors unseen in training (Kinect v1 and Kinect v2), the proposed model is more accurate than both the control model and the released metric Depth Anything V2 model in AbsRel and $\delta_1$; on NYUv2, for example, AbsRel is 0.088, against 0.133 for the control and 0.207 for the released model.}
